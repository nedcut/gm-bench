Why input tokens differ 9× between models: re-sent context, not reading

results/agentic_v21/*.jsondecision
Tried: explaining why mimo-v2.6-pro records 21.4M input tokens per game and gpt-6-astra 2.3M (9.3×) when their tool calls per game are 179 and 154 (1.2×). Every 2.1 row was compared on episodes[].usage (input_tokens, cached_input_tokens, output_tokens, api_calls, harness.compactions) and episodes[].agentic.tool_calls. Showed: each game is one harness session across all 20 phases. The model APIs behind these harnesses take the whole conversation on every request, so a session's input is the sum of its context size over all requests: it grows with how many requests a session makes and with everything that has piled up in it. The rows record only each game's totals, not what each request contained; the 92–99% cache-read share in every row is what a prefix re-sent request after request looks like. The OpenCode rows all have zero compactions. The five OpenCode rows' input per request tracks their output per game almost exactly (r = 0.998; each extra output token per game adds about 0.68 tokens to the average request), while their tool calls stay within 153–179. That fits a model's own replies and reasoning staying in the history and being re-sent on every later request. MiMo Pro writes 166k output tokens per game, Astra 7k. Within MiMo Pro's 32 games, input and output correlate at r = 0.81.

What the rows can't show

  • Codex reports completed turns, not model requests: api_calls is 1 per game. Astra's and Sol's context per request is unknown, and so is why Luna records 4.6× Astra's input on the same Codex build.
  • The rows keep one usage total per game, so "reasoning stays in the history" is inferred from the cross-model fit, not seen request by request.

Why it barely moves cost

  • 92–99% of every row's input is cache reads. Per game at list prices (api_equivalent_cost_usd), MiMo Pro costs $0.39 with 21.4M input and Astra $3.32 with 2.3M.
  • The token chart mostly measures session length and how much a model writes, not how much game data it read.
Decided: read the input-token axis as billed context (the whole session, re-sent on every request), not as data read. No change to the site for now: the Codex half is unexplained. Next step, in the driver files, not the contract: record per-request usage, or at least peak context per game, in each row, from OpenCode's session export (per message) and Codex's session rollouts (per turn). That would settle the Codex rows.

Provider timeouts: keep the official mean, disclose a no-timeout lift

results/agentic_v21/opencode-2.0.27-mimo-v2.6-flash-panel-32x5.jsonresults/agentic_v21/opencode-2.0.27-mimo-v2.6-pro-panel-32x5.jsondecision
Tried: publishing mimo-v2.6-flash and mimo-v2.6-pro (OpenCode 2.0.27 on OpenRouter, pinned to the Xiaomi host, fallbacks off, driver commit 1fb4597) on the 32-seed private panel under 2.1. The Xiaomi host was slow and congested during both panels. Showed: in 7 of 32 games of each panel, at least one phase hit the 1200-second phase guard and was closed without the agent (flash: 9 of 640 phases; pro: 7 of 640; telemetry.phases_ended_by). score-v2 has no timeout penalty, but a closed phase loses the decisions the agent would have made in it, so the stop costs the model points for a reason on the provider's side. Lift over pick-trader, paired by seed as the rows compute it: flash +18.7 (95% interval +6.4 to +30.9) on all 32 games, +21.8 (+7.6 to +35.3) on the 25 games with no phase-guard stop; pro +20.7 (+8.2 to +32.5) on all 32, +21.2 (+6.8 to +34.0) on its 25. The all-game figures are the rows' reference; the 25-game figures come from reference_contrast in gm_bench/agentic/publication.py run on the raw runs with the stopped games left out (the pairing needs the private seeds) and are published in the rows' notes in web/src/v2.ts. Leaving those games out raises each lift a little and changes no conclusion: both rows beat pick-trader either way.
Decided: a provider timeout is not removed from the official score. The row's score stays the mean of all 32 games, with the phase-guard stops counted as they happened, so every row is scored the same way and no game is dropped after its score is known. When a provider stalls the run, the row's note says so and gives the lift on the games with no phase-guard stop, paired by seed against pick-trader on those same games, as a disclosed sensitivity figure, not a second score.

Empty phases mostly measure a habit

  • The Codex models call get_status in every phase (20 per episode) and set_lineup in almost every phase (astra 18.7, sol 20.0). A phase with nothing to do is therefore not "empty" for them.
  • Opus and Sonnet skip get_status in 61% and 71% of phases. 82 of Opus's 88 empty phases and 81 of Sonnet's 97 have no status read at all.
  • 82 of Opus's 88 empty phases are drafts in which it held no pick (it had traded the pick away). Astra and sol give away more picks (115 and 124 of 160 drafts with no pick), but they log those drafts as non-empty.
  • Across the six rows, one draft was closed with a pick unused (Sonnet, in a non-empty phase).
  • Paired by seed, a Claude model's extra empty phases do not track its deficit: the correlation is -0.25 to +0.31 across the six OpenAI-Claude pairs.

The season-5 run-down is deliberate

  • In the season-5 preseason, astra and sol make 11.1 and 11.9 extensions per episode, Opus 2.9 and Sonnet 1.1.
  • All 140 of Opus's and all 149 of Sonnet's season-5 signings are one-year deals. 506 and 536 final-year players are left unextended when that preseason closes, against 55 for astra.
  • 21 Opus and 23 Sonnet episodes end with 5 or fewer players under contract.
  • The models say why: "the capped credit for future roster and picks will be low. I accepted that to maximise the five seasons that were scored" (Opus); "I did not extend those players because it was the last season" (Sonnet).
  • The brief calls this term "a capped credit". In score-v2 only cap room is clamped (-12 to +10). The asset, young-asset, pick, strength and depth terms are linear.
Also found (counts in behavior.json, filed in docs/bench_v2_1_queue.md):
  • The pick-trading window causes 128 of the 285 penalized rejections (45%). 115 of those 128 offered the current season's pick.
  • Draftees were left on the bench at draft close while outranking a dressed player at their position: Sonnet 116 of 135, deepseek 103 of 141, luna 87 of 149, Opus 27 of 77, against 3 of 39 for sol and 0 of 48 for astra. Sonnet set a lineup in none of its 160 drafts.
  • Models attempted 4 trades in the draft phase and 271 at the deadline, and signed no free agents in the draft phase.
Decided:
  • The empty-phase count mostly measures whether a model runs the get_status and set_lineup routine. It does not show whether a model neglected a decision. The site will relabel it and add two counts beside it: drafts closed while holding an unused pick, and phases ended without reading the status.
  • The brief's "capped" wording is a candidate cause of the season-5 run-down. It is filed with queue item 13.
  • The brief and tool fixes go to the next contract. 2.1's contract files stay frozen, and there is no paid rerun now.
Not reproduced: the lineup counterfactual, filling empty phases with cheap actions, and the fixed-effects regressions from the exploratory analysis. Their numbers are not used here.

When the GM holds two picks in one draft and uses one, 1.0 silently skips every opponent pick between them. pick-trader trips it in 29 of 32 private-panel episodes, so its whole league stays weaker. No published model episode ever triggered it. 2.1 fixes it.

How much headroom is above pick-trader?

docs/score_ceiling.mdresults/diagnostics/score_ceiling/
For one seed the simulator is a deterministic function of the GM's 20 decisions, so a per-seed optimum exists. We bracketed it with two searches: a clairvoyant beam search that sees the dice, and a fair planner that resamples everything a GM can't observe.

Lift over pick-trader · public seeds 101–124 (fair replicated on 125–172)

"Luck-neutral" swaps realized playoff results for their exact expectation. Seeing the dice is worth ~130 points, almost all of it playoffs. With playoff luck removed, clairvoyance is worth ~41 and a fair planner gets ~26 of it, ahead on 22 of 24 seeds and on 44 of 48 fresh seeds. About 64% of score variance turned out to be playoff dice, which led to score-v2.
